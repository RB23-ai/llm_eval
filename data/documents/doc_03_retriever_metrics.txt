Retriever quality in a RAG system is usually measured with recall and
precision at a cutoff K. Recall at K asks: out of all the documents that are
actually relevant to a query, how many did the retriever manage to place in
its top K results? Precision at K asks the reverse question: out of the K
documents the retriever returned, how many were actually relevant? Raising K
tends to increase recall, because there is more room for a relevant document
to appear, but it can lower precision by pulling in irrelevant filler that
adds noise to the context sent to the generator. Choosing K is therefore a
trade-off that should be tuned empirically against a labeled dataset of
queries and their known-relevant documents, rather than picked arbitrarily.
