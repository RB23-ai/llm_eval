The RAG triad is a way of evaluating a retrieval-augmented pipeline as a whole
by examining three relationships among the user question, the fetched
context, and the generated answer. Context relevance checks whether the
retrieved context actually relates to the question that was asked. Groundedness,
often called faithfulness, checks whether the generated answer is actually
supported by that retrieved context, rather than introduced from outside
knowledge or invented outright. Answer relevance checks whether the final
answer actually addresses what the user asked, since an answer can be fully
grounded in the context and still fail to respond to the actual question. A
pipeline needs all three relationships to hold at once; strong performance on
only one or two of them still leaves room for a poor user experience.
